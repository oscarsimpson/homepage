I tend to use bassa fillets, but other white fish or even salmon fillets would work just as well.

# Ingredients
- 2tbsp extra virgin olive oil
- 2 shallots or 1 medium white onion, finely diced or brunoised
- 150ml white wine
- fish stock cube: enough to make about 500ml of stock
- 1tsp dried tarragon
- 150ml double cream[^1]
- 1-2 tbsp capers
- 2x fish fillets
- 1tsp of dijon mustard, or juice of half a lemon
- 400ml can of butter beans
- 60g parsley, finely chopped

# Recipe

1. Gently saute the shallots in a large frying pan. Once softened, add the white wine and allow the alcohol to evaporate.
2. Break down the stock cube and mix in until dissolved. Add the tarragon and capers. Season to taste before adding the cream.
3. Lay the fish into the pan, add just enough water (if necessary) to come about halfway up the fish. Bring to a gentle simmer and lightly baste.
4. Cook for about 5 minutes[^2], basting occasionally. Flip the fish and cook for another 2 minutes or so. Remove the fish from the pan, allowing residual sauce to drain back into the pan, and leave to rest.
5. Add mustard and mix well. Turn the heat up to a vigorous simmer and allow the sauce to reduce until thickened.
6. Turn the heat back down, and add the butter beans and parsley.
7. Once the beans are warmed through, serve the fish on a bed of saucy beans, with some extra sauce spooned on top. Enjoy alone or with crusty bread.

[^1]: Single cream or even milk is acceptable, but you may need to use more to reach the desired richness.
[^2]: Cooking time will vary by fish and size of fillet. We are aiming for about 55C internal, but it will keep cooking while resting.
